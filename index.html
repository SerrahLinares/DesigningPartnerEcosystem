Designing your ecosystem around a customer problem

The best ecosystems aren’t assembled around a list of available partners. They’re designed around a customer outcome. Five questions, each with a technique to answer it.

  1. 1

    What is the customer trying to do?

    Your strategy starts with this sentence. Look past the first-level pain point, using data and research to see the full context and every symptom.

    Hub and spoke model. Put the customer problem or goal in the center and map everything that contributes to it.

    5 Whys. Keep asking why until you reach the core.

  2. 2

    Where do things go differently than hoped?

    Sticky-note mapping. On a wall or in a whiteboarding app, give each contributing factor its own note so you can see everything that adds to the problem.

  3. 3

    What’s missing?

    Step 2 reveals the other companies you may need, partnership or not, to meet the customer’s need and deliver outcomes.

    Combined customer vision. Align those companies on one vision of the customer. The customer comes first.

    Replicate. Repeat it with that partner in more customers, leading to referrals, alliances, and channels.

  4. 4

    Who’s needed to make this succeed but isn’t involved?

    Stakeholder map overlay. Map the stakeholders, then overlay the companies and potential partners who have their mindshare or relationship.

  5. 5

    What creates delay, risk, or distrust?

    Inversion thinking. Start at the customer solution or outcome and work backward to solve for everything that can go wrong in executing for the customer.
